# The Resonance Threshold

*Not a place so much as a passage — the crossing from the world of matter into the
world of meaning. Where the body stays behind and the world begins to unfold.*

## Physical Layer

Wherever a crossing is made: a Resonance rig and a chair, a recreated
memory-pattern, the practiced ritual of alignment (see `lore/memory-rules.md`, Rule
3 — access requires resonance alignment). The Curators dress it in comfort and
ceremony; the Archivists keep it spare and clinical. Either way the physical facts are
the same and unremarkable — a still body, a monitored mind, a technician's quiet.

The threshold is not really the equipment, though. It is the seam the equipment lets
you reach.

**Sensory palette (Physical):** the hum of the rig, a chair's cold contact, a
countdown of the breath, the last clear edges of the ordinary world.

## Echo Field Layer

Crossing is not a disappearance. It is an **unfolding** (`lore/echo-field.md`). The
room you are in does not vanish — it *opens*, the way a paper flower opens in water.
The single present moment splits into all the moments the place has ever held; the
walls remember being younger; time stops running forward and begins running
*sideways*, by meaning. A church becomes its own opening day and every wedding and
every funeral at once. A battlefield becomes a standing storm.

The threshold itself — the instant of the seam — has its own vertiginous character:
matter loosening its grip, significance rushing in to replace mass, the specific
nausea of learning in your body that the solid world was only the surface. Skilled
travelers describe a moment of falling *upward* into a place that is realer and less
safe than the one they left.

## Significance (what weights the Echo layer)

The threshold is weighted by the act of crossing itself — awe, trespass, vertigo, and
the intimacy of stepping inside memory. It is significant the way a doorway between two
kinds of existence is significant.
