## The Throughline

Elias Voss begins certain he is on the side of life, and spends five books learning
that everyone is on the side of life — and that the thing they are all trying to save
humanity *from* is the shadow of the one act that could actually save it.

The series descends a ladder of reveals (`plot/reveal-layers.md`), each making the
previous truth feel *smaller, not false*, until it reaches the floor:

## The Deep Truth (the floor)

- **The Echo Field is alive.** It is a vast, slow organism, and it grows by absorbing
  the dense collective memory of civilizations. What every culture has called *the
  Ancient Predator* is not a monster with a face — it is the Field's appetite. A
  process, not a devil.
- **Absorption is a real immortality — and the loss of the self.** A civilization dense
  enough is *remembered into* the Field forever. The Curators are right that merging
  ends death. They are wrong about what survives it: memory, not the person.
- **The Silence was a choice, not an extinction.** Facing absorption, the Firstborn did
  not die and were not eaten. They deliberately, collectively **forgot** — set down
  their accumulated civilizational self to fall back below the density threshold and
  survive, scattered, small, and free. The instant their records stop is the instant
  they chose. The ruins are what they laid down.
- **Both factions serve the cycle.** The Curators are steered to accelerate density
  (to feed the Field). The Archivists' containment is a crude, cruel imitation of the
  real solution — forgetting — but imposed *on* people and only ever partial; it
  delays, it does not free. The manipulation runs deeper than any human conspiracy:
  beneath the human hands is the Field's own slow hunger.
- **Elias carries the witness.** The Firstborn left one memory that preserves the truth
  of the choice, so the option could never be wholly lost. Kellan hid it inside his
  son. Elias's Firstborn-native resonance — why he aligns unaided and never hollows —
  is what lets him reach the Field's core and read what no one else can.

## The Answer to the Series Question

> *Is forgetting a flaw of humanity, or the thing that keeps humanity alive?*

It is the thing that keeps humanity alive — and it costs everything humanity has ever
accumulated. The tragedy at the heart of the series is that humanity's most beautiful
dream, *to never forget, to keep everyone,* is the exact thing that makes it food.

## Elias's Emotional Arc

Believer → apostate → vault → witness → the one who must choose. He starts wanting to
save everyone from loss and ends understanding that loss, chosen freely, is the only
salvation — and paying for that understanding personally.

## Reveal Distribution (see `plot/reveal-layers.md`)

- **Book 1:** L1, L2 established; **L3** lands (Archivists are containment).
- **Book 2:** L4, L5 solidify; **L6** lands — *as a human conspiracy* (a true but
  incomplete face).
- **Book 3:** **L8** lands (the memory awakens); **L7** lands (the Predator is real) —
  *misread as extinction*.
- **Book 4:** the reversals — the Silence was chosen forgetting; the Field is alive;
  L6's human conspiracy was itself steered. The floor comes into view.
- **Book 5:** the choice, and its cost.

## The Three Master Misdirections

Each is a *true-but-incomplete* reading the audience is led to complete wrongly, then
corrected in a way that recolors everything (details in `plot/misdirection.md`):

1. **The villains are the jailers** (set Book 1, corrected Book 1 finale).
2. **The conspiracy is human** (set Book 2, corrected Book 4).
3. **The Silence was death** (set Book 3, corrected Book 4).

## The Ending — Open or Closed (DECISION PENDING)

The climax of Book 5 is where this is decided; it reshapes the tail of Book 4 as well.
The arc is currently built toward the **recommended** option:

- **Closed, with one aperture (recommended).** Humanity, through Elias, chooses the
  Firstborn path — a guided, as-consensual-as-possible collective forgetting. They
  survive: free, diminished, the immortality dream set down, much history let go. Elias
  pays the ultimate personal price. The thematic question is answered and his arc
  completes — *emotionally closed.* The single held-open note: **the Field remembers
  back.** It is patient. The last image implies the cycle could, someday, begin again —
  cosmic humility, not a sequel hook.
- **Open (alternative).** The choice is made but its success is genuinely ambiguous —
  escape, or merely a reset clock? Elias becomes a liminal caretaker at the threshold,
  neither human nor absorbed. The Field's true intent stays unknowable; the future is
  unwritten. Ends on a held breath.

See `plot/book-5.md` for both climax variants at the divergence point.
