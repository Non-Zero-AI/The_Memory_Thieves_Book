# Mara Sennet

## Role

Elias's mother. Curator scientist and his first, deepest tie to the Curator cause.

She is the Curator argument made personal and sympathetic — not the movement's
ideology on a banner, but a mother who cannot accept that the people we love are
supposed to be lost. Through Book 1 she is Elias's emotional anchor and the wall his
doubts will eventually push against.

## Working Name

Mara Sennet (kept her own name; Elias carries his father's).

## Background

A brilliant memory scientist who came to the Curators through grief, not theory. She
watched her own mother disappear into dementia — a mind erased while the body
remained — and decided that forgetting, not death, was the true cruelty.

She raised Elias inside the Curator worldview so completely that he never thought to
question it. To Mara, teaching him to see forgetting as the enemy was an act of love.

## Faction and Belief

Curator. Her argument is the movement's strongest form because it is made of love
rather than ambition:

> No one should have to lose the people they love a piece at a time. We already
> know how to keep a mind. Refusing to is the only sin.

She is not naive about the cost. She simply believes the cost of *forgetting* is
worse, and she has the personal wound to make that belief unshakeable.

## Relationship to Memory and the Echo Field

A skilled Curator scientist specializing in consciousness-transfer research — the
work of merging a mind with stable Echo Field structures. She travels by Resonance
competently, though she is a researcher, not a field operative like Elias.

## The Secret

Mara chose to forget. When Kellan vanished, she accepted a comfortable version of
events and helped soften Elias's memories of his father "to protect him." She tells
herself this was mercy. She does not let herself examine how much she suspects was a
lie — or that in editing her son's grief, she may have helped hide what Kellan left
inside him.

## Voice

Warm, precise, tender. She speaks like a scientist who loves — clean explanations
wrapped around obvious feeling. Reaches for metaphors of keeping, light, and
preservation. Rarely raises her voice; her certainty does the work instead.

## Symptoms and Tells

- Deflects hard questions about Kellan with practiced ease
- Keeps no photographs of him, and has never explained why
- Grows quietest precisely when she is most afraid

## Character Arc

### Beginning
A devoted believer and a loving mother. Elias's safe harbor and the human face of
everything the Curators promise.

### Middle
Her certainty becomes an obstacle as Elias learns things that don't fit. The more he
doubts, the more she insists — and the insistence starts to look like fear.

### Later
Forced to confront what she chose not to know about Kellan, and what her editing of
Elias's childhood concealed.

### Final Direction
A tragic figure of the Curator dream: a woman whose love was real and whose belief
may have helped doom the thing she loved most. Her arc asks whether preserving
someone and protecting them are the same act.
